title: "MeasureCamp"
description: |
  MeasureCamp is an unconference for the data and analytics community: a free one-day event
  with no programme announced in advance, where the schedule is built on the morning by the
  people who turned up. This model describes how one runs. Each edition is put on by its own
  local volunteer committee in one city on one day; it opens with a session board — a grid
  of rooms against time slots — and attendees claim a place on it by writing a session card
  and pinning it up. Around that sit the things that make the day possible: the tickets that
  are the only way in, the people behind them, the committee roles that had to be filled,
  and the sponsors whose money pays for the venue and whose names end up on the rooms.

  The model is built around two distinctions that are easy to lose and expensive to get
  wrong. Registering is not attending — a ticket is a claim on a place and an attendee is
  someone who took it, and the gap between the two is what an organiser wants to know before
  the day. And the board's size is a decision, not an outcome: rooms times session slots is
  how many sessions the day could hold, which is the denominator behind the standard
  post-event questions — was the board full, and was there enough variety on it.

  **Scope:** this is the shape of the event, not a ledger and not an audience tracker.
  Sponsorship is the only money in it and there are no costs, so whether an edition covered
  its outgoings cannot be answered here. Nor is attendance at individual sessions recorded:
  who led a session is known, who sat in it is not.

# Example Questions

- How full did each board get — of the `rooms` times session slots a `session board` offered, how many carried an actual `session`, and which editions left the most space unused?
- Was the programme varied or lopsided — how did an edition's `sessions` spread across level, type and audience, and did any board end up with nothing for beginners?
- How far is registering from attending — what share of `tickets` turned into `attendees`, how many of those were first-timers, and does the gap differ by `city`?
